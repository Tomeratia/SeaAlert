SeaAlert: Robust Severity Classification and LLM-Based Information Extraction for Noisy Maritime Distress Communications

Tomer Atia1, Yehudit Aperstein2, Alexander Apartsin1
1School of Computer Science, HIT - Holon Institute of Technology, Israel
2Intelligent Systems, Afeka Academic College of Engineering, Tel Aviv, Israel
Published in IEEE Access · 2026 · DOI: 10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3709004
Paper Code Data

SeaAlert is a controlled experimental framework for evaluating robust analysis of maritime distress communications. It combines LLM-based synthetic data generation with VHF noise simulation, ASR transcription, transformer-based severity classification, and LLM-based structured information extraction to assess robustness under realistic operational conditions.

Abstract

Maritime distress communications over VHF radio are safety-critical voice messages governed by the Global Maritime Distress and Safety System (GMDSS). In practice, automatic analysis remains difficult because messages are often brief, noisy, and produced under stress, may deviate from the prescribed format, and are further degraded by automatic speech recognition (ASR) errors caused by channel noise and speaker stress.

This paper presents SeaAlert, a controlled experimental framework for evaluating robust analysis of maritime distress communications using transformer-based severity classification and LLM-based structured extraction. To address the scarcity of labeled real-world data, we develop a synthetic data generation pipeline in which an LLM produces diverse maritime messages, including challenging variants in which standard distress codewords are omitted or replaced with less explicit expressions. The generated utterances are synthesized into speech, degraded with simulated VHF noise, and transcribed by an ASR system to obtain controlled noise-degraded transcripts.

The resulting evaluation shows that transformer-based classification (RoBERTa) degrades more gracefully than lexical baselines under ASR noise and codeword masking, while LLM-based extraction (GPT-4o-mini) is more effective than Regex-based extraction for noisy structured fields.

Methodology

SeaAlert addresses two goals: Severity Classification (Routine / Safety / Urgency / Distress) and Information Extraction (Vessel, Call Sign, MMSI, Position, POB, Nature of Incident) from noisy ASR transcripts. A synthetic dataset of maritime radio messages, generated by GPT-4o-mini, is synthesized into speech, degraded with simulated VHF channel noise, and transcribed by Whisper ASR to obtain realistic noisy transcripts.

We compare a rule-based GMDSS keyword spotter, a TF-IDF + Logistic Regression baseline, and a fine-tuned RoBERTa model across four experiments: ASR robustness, adversarial scenarios, codeword-masking ablation, and Regex vs. GPT-4o-mini structured extraction.

Key Findings

Experiment Rule-Based GMDSS Spotter BoW (Logistic Regression) RoBERTa
Clean Text Accuracy 57.3% 68.0% 67.6%

ASR Medium Macro-F1 — 0.427 0.619
ASR High Macro-F1 — 0.423 0.595
Relative Drop (Clean → ASR High) — ~37% ~11%
ECE at ASR High (lower is better) — 0.236 0.068
Adversarial Trap Accuracy (n=15) — 26.7% 40.0%

Takeaway: Clean-text performance is comparable across models, but RoBERTa's real advantage emerges under degradation - a much smaller Macro-F1 drop, better-calibrated confidence, and stronger fallback behavior when GMDSS codewords are masked or removed. For structured extraction, GPT-4o-mini zero-shot surpasses Regex on every field at high ASR noise, especially alphanumeric identifiers (MMSI, Call Sign). Both classifiers still fail on drill-message traps, underscoring the need for human oversight.
